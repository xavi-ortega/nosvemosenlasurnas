1. Scope and user journey

Build both a programme explorer and a short policy questionnaire for the Congress election first. Both are free, with no account required.

  • Define “all” using the official confirmed candidacy register, rather than a hand-picked list of national parties. Until proclamation, label the registry provisional. Reconcile it after official publication and subsequent corrections or appeals.
  • Include parties, coalitions and elector groups by their actual ballot identity. Link shared programmes once and keep distinct local annexes. Do not merge coalition members’ positions unless the joint programme supports doing so.
  • Maintain a nationwide browse mode. Let users select a Congress constituency locally to see ballot availability; do not request an address, postcode, GPS location or voter identification.
  • Two equal entry points: (1) compare parties by topic with no questionnaire; (2) take the questionnaire and explore overlaps. Comparison includes proposals, conditions, quantified commitments where present, original citations and unknowns. Optional constituency selection and priorities remain local. Optional feedback is separately explained.
  • Deliver mobile web access, keyboard and screen-reader support, and Spanish as the default interface across public and editorial screens. Keep Catalan, Basque and Galician interfaces in scope; enable each only after its translations and political wording have been approved by fluent reviewers. Preserve exact original-language source quotations with separately labelled translations. All original application code and developer documentation use English.
  • MVP excludes tactical-voting advice, seat predictions, candidate personality rankings, historical-record fact checking, visitor accounts, political advertising, and social leaderboards.
  • Free means no payment for the questionnaire, comparisons, citations, results or ordinary exports. The project will publish its original application code and methodology under an open-source licence. Publish original editorial metadata and explanations under a suitable separate licence where rights permit; preserve rights and attribution for third-party programmes. Keep funding transparent; no political advertising or sale of political response data.
  • Congress-first matching is the current proposed release scope, shown clearly throughout the product. Archive relevant programme documents with their actual chamber and territorial scope. Keep any Senate availability and later matching separate; a Congress-only registry must not be advertised as covering all ballot options in both chambers.
  • Baseline access is public, free and without visitor accounts. “Private” means political answers and results remain local and metrics follow a separate approved flow; it does not mean the public evidence website has a private login. Editorial accounts are required and restricted. Invitation gating is outside the baseline scope.
  • Capability-based releases are explicit: registry/source explorer can publish first, the private questionnaire activates after its own evidence/method/privacy gates, reviewed regional locales activate for available surfaces, and metrics remain optional. Public badges describe what is actually enabled, not the eventual whole-project ambition.
  • The questionnaire stays party-neutral until the user explicitly finishes; the independent public programme explorer remains available without a quiz. After finish, provide scoped overlap insights and exact cited explanations for answered questions, plus further public policy information.

2. What “truth” means in the product

Separate source evidence, interpretation and real-world verification.

  • Source evidence: “The programme proposes X”, with an exact excerpt and locator. This is the primary product claim.
  • Interpretation: “This proposal supports the questionnaire statement.” Display the reasoning and the evidence status. A scoring judgement is an interpretation even when carefully reviewed.
  • Real-world verification: whether a promise is feasible, funded or delivered needs a separate evidence base. Do not present a manifesto quotation as proof that a factual claim is correct or that a promise will happen.
  • Use equal inclusion, wording, topic weighting, evidence rules and review standards for every candidacy. Equal standards do not require equal scores or inventing an opposing position when the evidence is absent.
  • Use neutral names and visual treatment, explain the method, disclose funding and editorial conflicts, and maintain a correction log. Parties may submit source corrections through the same process; they do not receive editorial vetoes.
  • Have two independent reviewers approve every scored position and question. Keep a named adjudication role for disagreement. Readers can challenge an excerpt or interpretation through a form that does not require revealing their quiz answers.
  • Separate source acquisition, position assessment, independent approval and release publication permissions. Record reviewer conflicts, independence and disputes. Two accounts owned by one person do not meet the review requirement. An adjudicator cannot silently waive evidence or quorum; unresolved mappings stay unscored.

3. Collect and version the programmes

The evidence workflow is likely to require more work than the questionnaire UI.

  • Create a source register per candidacy: official programme URL, election, geographical scope, language, publication date if known, retrieval timestamp, version and availability status. Distinguish “not found”, “not yet published”, “published but unreviewed” and “reviewed”. Do not imply every candidacy will publish a programme.
  • Prefer official party or coalition websites and verified programme documents. A 2023 programme, news article or campaign interview is not a substitute for a 2026 programme. Keep supplementary commitments and historical material separately labelled and outside the default score.
  • Save an immutable original and SHA-256 hash, subject to publication and reuse rights. Extract text with OCR where needed. Retain PDF page index, printed page label, section, paragraph and optional bounding box. For HTML, preserve a timestamped snapshot and stable text locator.
  • Each scored position must carry a short exact quotation, surrounding context, original language, separately labelled translation if used, original URL, page/section, document hash and review record.
  • AI may propose passages, translations, summaries and question mappings from public documents. Human reviewers approve them against the original. Never send user answers or inferred matches to an AI provider.
  • Detect changes against the source register during development and operations. Re-review changed passages; publish a new version of the dataset and mappings. Preserve old releases so a result can be reproduced with its original version.
  • Do not claim complete programme coverage until the registry has been reconciled and every candidacy has an explicit source status. A missing programme stays visible in browsing and is marked as insufficient evidence for scoring.
  • Availability assessment as of 7 October: spot checks established access to historical PSOE and PP programme PDFs and a Sumar 23J programme page. They did not establish a complete set of official programmes for November 2026. The election was called only recently; keep 2023 material clearly historical and outside the current-election score.
  • Define reusable programme scopes before expanding the matrix: one national programme may support many ballot candidacies, while territorial annexes or joint coalition texts require their own applicability records. Reuse approved evidence only where the exact document, question and scope are identical; never inherit a member party’s stance into a coalition without joint-source support.
  • A release validator checks source authenticity, election, chamber and territorial applicability, quotation accuracy, context, locator resolution, document hash, rights status and independent approvals. Hashes detect changed bytes; they do not establish that a document is authentic or legally redistributable.
  • Fetch only approved public sources. Bound size, redirects, elapsed time and retries; protect against private-network/metadata URLs and DNS rebinding. Parse/OCR PDFs and snapshots in an isolated process with resource limits. Treat extracted HTML, embedded links and AI-facing document instructions as untrusted content.
  • Record last successful source check, last document change and last review separately. Broken or changed external links do not silently invalidate an already archived reviewed quotation; mark access status, verify authenticity and revise the release when interpretation changes.
  • A not-stated position means no explicit position was found under the documented review of the applicable programme. Record review/search extent and OCR limitations; do not claim exhaustive absence from a quick search. A missing or unreviewed programme is a source-availability status, not a reviewed assertion that the party has no policy.

4. Progressive questions without forced choices

Ten accessible balanced starter questions, followed by optional five-question blocks from a larger reviewed bank.

  • A: strongly agree. B: somewhat agree. C: somewhat disagree. D: strongly disagree. Add separate “neither agree nor disagree”, “I am unsure” and “skip” choices. Neutral is a real answer; uncertainty and skipping are not scored.
  • Propose a reviewed bank of 60–100 distinct policy questions, subject to actual evidence and review capacity, across 8–10 domains: housing, taxation and public spending, work and pensions, health and care, education, energy and climate, migration, territorial governance, rights and institutions, and EU/foreign policy. These are an initial editorial proposal, not a validated question bank.
  • Choose specific policy distinctions supported by the collected corpus. Avoid slogans, assumptions of bad intent, leading preambles, two policies in one question, and choices that bundle unrelated beliefs.
  • Keep ABCD as answer labels, not ideological categories. Do not presume A is left-wing or D is right-wing. If a question asks people to choose between different policies, use a separately published compatibility rubric rather than pretending the letters form an ordered scale.
  • Design examples only: “El Estado debería limitar las subidas del alquiler en las zonas declaradas tensionadas.” “España debería fijar una fecha de cierre para las centrales nucleares existentes.” “Deberían ampliarse las vías legales de entrada para trabajar en España.” No party position is asserted for these examples; remove or revise them unless the 2026 corpus supports useful, fair comparisons.
  • Ask importance by domain, optionally, using simple levels such as normal, important and very important. Publish equal-domain defaults. Let users change priorities locally; avoid repeatedly counting essentially the same policy.
  • Pilot for comprehension, completion burden, question-order effects, translation consistency, perceived neutrality and how scores change when a question is removed. Review sensitive or misunderstood wording before release.
  • Before freezing questions, publish the topic budget and selection criteria; measure overlap and missingness across programme scopes. Pilot blinded independent coding, conditions/negation, duplicate issue coverage, partly answered topics and regional translations. Use synthetic response profiles for scoring sensitivity. Do not retain participants’ real answer vectors, ideology labels or voting intentions to conduct the pilot.
  • The first checkpoint is after ten presented questions, with actual numeric-answer count separate from skipped/unsure responses. It shows progress, optional policy-permitted highest overlap without identifying a party, and stop/+5 choices; no party-specific score mapping. Only an explicit finish reveals cited results; users can also leave without revealing.
  • Adaptive ordering selects useful reviewed distinctions and fills topic gaps locally. It never guesses unanswered beliefs, chooses a target party or uses acceptance metrics as its objective.
  • The bank size is an editorial capacity proposal, not a quota. Every scored mapping still requires exact current-election evidence and two genuinely independent approvals.

5. Transparent matching and overlaps

Use an inspectable five-point policy-distance calculation, with known-evidence overlap, weighted coverage and explicitly shared comparison sets.

  • Detailed method version: policy-distance-v1.0.0. User answers use −2, −1, 0, +1, +2; unsure and skip have no numeric value. Party positions use a published commitment/support rubric rather than inferred emotional intensity. Only reviewed current-election programme positions are scored.
  • Normalize question base weights within each answered topic and normalize the user’s topic priorities across answered topics. Keep those original weights fixed when assessing each party’s evidence coverage. Topics have equal default influence regardless of question count.
  • Per-question similarity is 1 − |user code − party code| / 4. For known positions, K is the sum of weight × similarity and C is the known weight. Report 100 × K/C as an index out of 100, alongside 100 × C evidence coverage. An index is not the probability of affiliation or the literal proportion of manifesto promises accepted.
  • For missing party positions, the smallest possible similarity is 1 − max(|user code + 2|, |user code − 2|)/4; the largest is 1. Add those weighted possible contributions to K to form a full-question lower/upper bound. These are coding-based missing-evidence bounds, not statistical confidence intervals. With C = 0, show an unavailable score.
  • Direct comparisons use the exact same answered questions with reviewed positions for every member of the explicit comparison cohort. Use the same original weights, renormalized by their common total. Do not silently exclude candidates with poor documentation or combine comparisons using different question sets into one ordering.
  • Initial pilot thresholds for a roughly 30-question release: 20 scored user answers over 6 topics, 15 common questions over 6 topics, and 80% common weighted coverage. A proposed 3-point grouping convention avoids overstating near scores. These settings must be reviewed, piloted, published and versioned; they are not validated scientific thresholds.
  • A pure browser-compatible reference engine and clearly fictional arithmetic fixtures now implement this proposal. Fifteen meaningful verification groups pass. Real programmes, question selection, party codings, privacy integration and the production Laravel app remain unvalidated development work.
  • The all-candidacy shared intersection may be empty or too narrow even when many programmes are available. Treat this as an evidence limitation, not an engineering defect. Keep every candidacy visible, show documented overlaps and missing evidence, and permit explicitly selected comparisons only under the published policy. Do not promise a universal best-party result.
  • Partly answered topics retain their chosen topic priority distributed among answered questions. Explain this behaviour and show response coverage per topic. Pilot sensitivity to skipped answers, one answered question in a high-priority topic, leave-one-question-out changes and alternate ordinal coding; publish material fragility without calling it a statistical confidence interval.
  • Before release, check finite bounded numeric weights, finite normalized totals, similarity/index bounds, unique IDs, approval metadata, schema compatibility and the comparison cohort. Detect arithmetic/schema failures before publication and withhold personal scores on an incompatible or corrupt bundle.
  • The adaptive flow is specified separately as adaptive-questionnaire-v0.2.0-proposed. Ten-question output is provisional; existing 20-answer/15-common-question reference thresholds stay explicit until a separately validated short-mode policy is approved.
  • Preferred adaptive weighting fixes topic/family/question budgets across the entire pinned bank. This is a proposed method change requiring a new reviewed algorithm version; the v1 reference and its 15 checks remain unchanged.
  • Response coverage, manifesto evidence coverage and deterministic result stability are separate. Never present an invented confidence percentage or a probability that a person belongs to a party.
  • Stopping bounds must use the same scoring target/weights as the published adaptive method. The fixed-bank diagnostic cannot certify v1's changing answered-only weighting. Identical parties, missing programme evidence or skipped answers can prevent a stronger conclusion.
  • Overlap strength is the main permitted result after explicit finish; coverage/stability are supporting information. Indices are independent rather than shares summing to 100%, can rise or fall with new answers, and never identify a person's party or prescribe a vote. Insufficient common evidence suppresses a broad highest-match headline.

6. Privacy architecture

Private is a set of testable data-flow rules, not an assurance that no technical trace can ever exist.

  • Questionnaire answers, importance settings, constituency and results stay in browser memory by default. Private reflection is also local unless the person separately opts into an approved anonymous metrics flow. Clear resets the session; reload starts again. Saving to the device or exporting requires an explicit action and explains shared-device risk.
  • Serve the same complete reviewed election bundle to every user, independent of their answers or selected constituency. Run deterministic scoring in the browser. Source links are user-initiated. Offer an offline bundle for users who need to avoid hosting access traces.
  • Core servers store public-source documents, editorial records and released data, with no questionnaire response database. Any approved metrics pipeline is separately operated and outputs aggregates into a restricted dashboard. It may need short-lived encrypted protocol records while aggregating, followed by deletion; do not describe that as zero processing. Editorial admin access is separate and restricted. Invitation identity must never be linked to quiz responses.
  • Self-host fonts and assets. Exclude advertising, tracking pixels, third-party analytics, session replay, remote questionnaire APIs and behavioural recommendation systems. Keep answers out of URLs, referrers, crash reports, breadcrumbs and logs.
  • Document hosting, CDN and security-log behaviour. Disable unnecessary logs and identifiers, restrict unavoidable security data, minimize retention and verify providers’ configuration. The host necessarily handles connection metadata while online; do not promise absolute anonymity.
  • Use transport security, restrictive browser content policy, dependency review, safe rendering of extracted text, sanitised document handling and audited administrative access. Treat fetched programmes as untrusted input to both extraction and AI workflows.
  • Political opinions are specially protected under GDPR Article 9. Spain’s LOPDGDD Article 9 adds restrictions on consent for identifying ideology. Obtain specialist review of the actual data flows, lawful bases and DPIA requirements before launch; client-side processing reduces exposure but does not automatically resolve every legal question.
  • Publish an accurate privacy notice and data-flow inventory. Public editorial methodology may coexist with private user responses. Metrics participation is optional and declining it preserves the full free service. The plan and any prototype remain local unless separately authorised for deployment.
  • Privacy claims apply precisely to local political inputs. Public page/source navigation can reveal ordinary browsing metadata to hosting or external party sites. Disclose this distinction, suppress referrers and avoid automatic remote document embeds. Show the archived accessible excerpt locally; external originals open only after an intentional click.
  • Inventory non-quiz personal data separately: editorial accounts and reviewer records, optional correction contact details and necessary restricted security logs. Give each a purpose, access policy, retention rule and deletion process. Public professional reviewer attribution is a separate governance choice, not a reason to link quiz responses.
  • Inspect browser autofill, back/forward cache, reload, language switching, clear/reset, downloads and accidental crash paths. No answer persistence by default. Keep caches/service workers limited to public assets if introduced; offline packages contain public code/data only unless a user deliberately exports personal results.
  • Declare political inputs as sensitive in engineering reviews. Prohibit automatic error-report payloads, analytics breadcrumbs and support attachments containing answers or affinities. Public issue templates ask for synthetic reproduction steps, never a person’s political profile.
  • Public application routes must not serialize political state into query strings, form submissions or full-request logs. Use browser event handling that prevents accidental form/Enter submissions and verify that actual hosting/error middleware does not capture sensitive payloads. Test keyboard and unexpected-error paths, not only deliberate clicks.

7. Useful metrics that respect the promise

Include useful anonymous metrics as a planned feature. Keep collection disabled until the actual anonymity and legal design have been verified.

  • Three metric groups: editorial completeness and quality; coarse service usage; optional post-result feedback. Editorial metrics use public documents and review records and require no personal response collection.
  • Editorial metrics: source-status coverage by candidacy, reviewed question-position coverage, broken source links, review disagreements, update age and correction turnaround. Calculate these from each dataset release.
  • Optional usage counters: questionnaire starts and completions, comparison visits, citation opens and generic failures. Send no party, question ID, selected topic, constituency, answer vector, result, browser fingerprint, account or persistent visitor ID. Explain collection before participation. Ratios based on these counters describe events, not reliably unique people.
  • Optional post-result questions: “Was the comparison clear?”; “Does it reflect your policy preferences?”; “Would you consider voting differently after reviewing this?” Include unsure and prefer-not-to-answer options. For willingness to reconsider, ask no intended party or matched party. Participants can submit one feedback item or skip all of them.
  • Proposed measures: clarity share and perceived-accuracy share among submitted feedback responses, with separate unsure counts; willingness-to-reconsider share among that question’s responding submissions; and the approximate completion-event ratio. Specify numerator, denominator, consent scope, release version and time period. Do not label these figures as national opinion, unique voters, causal effects or verified changes in votes.
  • Start with coarse global totals or non-overlapping weekly batches, not demographic, regional or per-party breakdowns. Keep the dashboard private by default. Suppress small cells, restrict repeated overlapping queries, document sampling and duplicate-submission limitations, and apply an assessed differential-privacy policy where appropriate. A sample-size threshold alone does not establish anonymity.
  • For sensitive feedback, evaluate a maintained secure-aggregation implementation with two independently controlled aggregators and a collector that receives only approved batch totals. The IETF DAP design is a relevant work-in-progress reference. Under its honest-aggregator assumption, no aggregator sees an individual measurement in the clear. Infrastructure operators must not be able to collude through shared administrative access.
  • Secure aggregation protects measurement values; it does not by itself hide source IPs, timing or contribution patterns. Assess an independent relay, log configuration, task metadata, batch-selection attacks, aggregate differencing, retention and shared-operator risks. Use an established implementation and specialist review; do not invent cryptography.
  • Permit only a fixed metric schema. Delete encrypted per-report state after a documented batch lifecycle; discard undersized batches at their expiry rather than retaining them indefinitely. Keep no recoverable questionnaire responses. Final retention periods and batch sizes must follow the threat model and operational tests.
  • Opt-in is a product choice, not proof of a lawful basis or true anonymity. Obtain the applicable privacy and legal assessment before collection. If the metrics design is not ready, launch the free questionnaire and comparison with editorial metrics and local feedback; add collection once verified.
  • Do not optimize wording for flattering matches or changed votes. No political targeting, per-party persuasion dashboard, sale of profiles, or training on user responses. If electoral survey findings are ever published, assess LOREG Article 69 restrictions separately.
  • All non-editorial measurement is disabled by default in the baseline deployment. Optional usage and post-result feedback activate only after the fixed schema, transport, operators, consent, legal basis, retention and aggregation-release rules pass their separate gate. The core launches without them if necessary.
  • Do not resolve anonymous-count abuse by quietly introducing IP hashes, fingerprinting or persistent identifiers. Label event/submission denominators honestly and document bot/repeat-submission uncertainty. Choose abuse controls only after assessing their privacy tradeoffs; publish no population or unique-voter estimates from self-selected feedback.
  • Private dashboard access does not establish anonymous collection. Public aggregate publication is outside baseline scope; any later publication requires an additional legal and statistical review, including electoral-survey rules. No per-party or per-constituency persuasion statistics.

10. Release gates and meaningful verification

Publish only the behaviour that the evidence and privacy checks support.

  • Completeness: every confirmed candidacy is represented with ballot availability and a source status. Every scored question-position cell has an approved excerpt and review history. No unpublished or historical programme is silently substituted.
  • Evidence: two reviewers can reproduce the interpretation from the original document; conditional wording is retained; page links resolve; OCR and translations have been checked. The dataset, rubric and corrections log are versioned.
  • Scoring: test missing, ambiguous and conflicting evidence; neutral versus skipped answers; domain weighting; near ties; and reproducibility. Identical positions must yield identical scores. Reordering parties must not change results. Unknown positions must never become opposition or neutrality.
  • Coverage: check interval bounds against complete synthetic cases and extremes; verify common-question comparisons use the same weights and evidence set; suppress overall ranking when evidence is inadequate.
  • Privacy: inspect network traffic while answering, changing weights, viewing results, resetting and exporting. Questionnaire answers, matches and constituency choices must stay local. Optional reflection contributions may leave only through the separately explained, consented and verified aggregation flow. Test consent refusal, error paths and logs against these rules.
  • Usability and neutrality: test mobile and keyboard flows, screen-reader labels, clarity of unknown evidence, citation comprehension and a pilot spanning differing political preferences without retaining ideology profiles.
  • Governance: publish ownership and funding, settle reviewer disputes, complete the relevant privacy/legal review, and assign an owner and turnaround target for corrections. Maintain a way to withdraw a disputed mapping promptly.
  • Metrics release gate: verify the real collection flow, transport metadata, operator separation, minimum batch and expiry behaviour, deletion, privacy-budget/query limits, denominators, consent and legal assessment. Do not advertise anonymity solely because a vendor or library uses that label.
  • Open-source verification: release the scoring code, method and reviewed evidence bundle; link the deployed code commit and data release; provide repeatable build instructions and artifact hashes; independently verify the served browser assets where possible. A server-displayed version string alone does not prove the production deployment.
  • Language release gate: Spanish is complete across public/editorial screens, accessibility attributes, errors, downloads and user-facing documentation. Every enabled regional locale must also have complete, fluent-reviewed interface and question translations. CI checks locale key coverage, interpolation/plural forms, prohibited hardcoded UI strings and unresolved keys; browser checks cover normal, empty and failure paths. Review original code identifiers, comments, schemas and developer documentation for English. Verify switching locale preserves scores and citations.
  • Decision status: public free access, Spanish default, reviewed regional interfaces, English code and local deterministic matching are baseline choices. Operator identity, staffing, licences, budget/providers, final comparison thresholds and optional aggregation operators remain unresolved. Resolve each required decision before its dependent task; do not label planning decisions as completed implementation.
  • Readiness is evidence-driven. Requirements REQ-01 through REQ-18, tasks TASK-01 through TASK-15, risks and decisions below define verification and fallback behaviour. Owner labels are proposed roles; actual accountable people must be named before beta or deployment.
  • The explicit, strict GATE-00–GATE-08 records below control execution. Earlier checklist gates remain acceptance criteria, not independent permission to deploy.

14. Do we need machine learning?

No custom trained model or model-serving infrastructure is needed for matching or comparison.

  • The matching algorithm is a published distance calculation over user answers, domain weights and reviewed party positions. It is deterministic and can be tested with synthetic cases. It does not learn a person’s ideology from other users.
  • The comparison tool reads structured, reviewed proposals and citations. Ordinary topic filtering and browser text search are enough for the first release; embeddings and a vector database are optional later conveniences.
  • Useful optional AI assistance happens in the editorial pipeline: suggesting relevant passages, draft summaries, proposed question mappings, translation drafts and source changes. Require original-document verification before anything is published. Conventional OCR may itself use ML, but that does not require training our own political classifier.
  • Never score from an unreviewed generated summary or auto-fill a missing party position. Do not send user responses to model providers, fine-tune on them, or train models to maximize result acceptance or willingness to switch votes.
  • A natural-language chatbot would add inference cost, citation-validation work and opportunities for unsupported claims. It is outside the first release. We can keep the core useful and free through reviewed search, comparisons and local scoring.
  • Most critical work is editorial: defensible question choice, accurate evidence mapping, treatment of missing information and independent review. More sophisticated ML does not solve those judgement and governance requirements.
  • Laravel Boost is AI tooling for development, installed as a development dependency. It does not require adding Rubix ML, an electoral training dataset or a user-facing prediction model.

Open source lets people inspect and reproduce behaviour. It cannot prove personal intentions, neutral editorial judgement or private server practices by itself.

  • Publish the original Laravel code, browser scoring module, data schemas, question-selection method, weighting and missing-evidence rules. Publish synthetic scoring fixtures and meaningful invariance tests so reviewers can reproduce results without using real people’s answers.
  • Publish the reviewed public-evidence dataset with exact source references, programme versions, position interpretations, uncertainty states and correction history.   • Tie every deployed release to a source commit, dependency lockfiles, algorithm version and evidence-bundle hash. Supply repeatable build and self-hosting instructions.   • Provide a clearly labelled source/release link in the product and an offline or local mode. People should be able to inspect the scoring implementation and run the questionnaire without submitting responses to the operator.
  • Show the same reviewed dataset and scoring code to all users. Preserve source and data release history. Announce changes to wording, weights or party mappings with their reasons so later reviewers can assess whether edits favour a party.

20. Release integrity, operations and fallback

Publish a coherent version, verify failure paths, and demonstrate recovery before promising campaign-period availability.

  • Release state sequence: draft → reviewed → validated → staged → active; old releases become superseded or withdrawn. Only an approved active manifest points visitors to a release. Publication requires independent editorial approval, compatible schema and successful acceptance checks.
  • Manifest fields: release ID/time/status, source commit, dependency lockfiles/build provenance, schema/questionnaire/rubric/algorithm versions, pinned comparison policy, registry version, enabled locales, browser/public asset hashes, evidence-bundle hash and editorial approval record. Hashes verify byte integrity; they do not themselves prove editorial truth or trustworthy delivery.
  • The manifest declares enabled capabilities (source explorer, questionnaire, locales and any approved metrics). Explorer-only releases explicitly disable questionnaire and mark its version fields not applicable. Enabling a capability requires compatible assets and additional acceptance proofs; no feature turns on just because its code exists.
  • Build from a clean locked environment. Generate immutable content-addressed code/data assets, validate every referenced position/quotation/translation, test the exact staged artefacts and activate the manifest atomically. A partially uploaded bundle or stale mixed cache must fail with localized safe copy, never partial political results.
  • Pin one release when a questionnaire starts. New source/code releases do not silently alter that in-memory session; offer an explicit restart or refresh with a notice. Local exports record compatible versions and explain that political content leaves the device only through the user’s deliberate export.
  • Correct a disputed source with a documented, versioned change. Mark or withdraw affected mappings immediately when the evidence cannot support them. Roll back to a compatible verified release, or disable scoring if the earlier release shares the defect. Preserve accessible history with withdrawn status rather than erasing it.
  • Already downloaded/offline packages cannot be retroactively recalled. They show their source version and last-known status; an online check may offer a corrected public package without submitting answers. Do not promise that an offline old result always knows about later withdrawal.
  • Proposed performance/recovery targets in section 13 require a named traffic/device profile and recorded load/restore drill before acceptance. Snapshot backup consistency, verify hashes and access permissions after restoration, and document who can publish or roll back.
  • Monitor uptime, source/extraction failures, review backlog, release integrity, storage/cost thresholds and ordinary server health. Monitoring must exclude answer payloads and affinity results. Publish actual supported correction/on-call hours and ownership before launch.

Test each advertised fallback.
  • Set budget alerts against the chosen funded ceiling and pause nonessential extraction/AI/metrics jobs at agreed thresholds. Verify provider quota/overage behaviour; do not advertise free hosting or a hard spend cap without evidence. Public user access stays free.
  • Any later public aggregate feedback publication is a separate feature decision with actual-flow/legal/statistical review. The default private dashboard does not waive collection or publication requirements.
